# What is a Cabinet?

A cabinet is <mark data-color="yellow">**a folder on your computer + an app for working with it**</mark>. The folder holds your knowledge. The app gives you three ways to work with it.

Open Cabinet and you see three tabs — the same three you'll see across these docs.

## The three sides of a Cabinet

**<span class="tx-amber">Data.</span>** Your file tree. Notes, PDFs, spreadsheets, code, diagrams, images, embedded apps — everything in one browseable folder, with a rich markdown editor and first-class viewers. → [Read more about Data](./data/)

**<span class="tx-purple">Agents.</span>** Your AI team. Personas with goals, skills, and memory who live in the same folder as your work. Bring your own model. → [Read more about Agents](./agents/)

**<span class="tx-accent">Tasks.</span>** What the team does. One-off tasks, scheduled routines, and recurring heartbeats — outputs land back in your folder as durable pages. → [Read more about Tasks](./tasks/)

## Why the three together

A scattered tool stack means scattered context. An assistant in ChatGPT can't see your meeting notes. An agent in Linear can't see your research. A scheduled job in Zapier can't write back into your brain.

<span class="tx-green">Cabinet's bet is that knowledge, the workforce, and the work all have to live in the same folder for any of it to compound.</span>
